E-House

E-House Solutions

Prefabricated, climate-controlled power buildings that receive, protect and distribute power for your site. We engineer, integrate, test and commission every e-house in a controlled factory, then verify it on site so it is ready to energize.

Interactive 3D

Walk inside an e-house

Approach the building, step through the door and see every component explained, from medium-voltage switchgear to the battery room.

Key Benefits

  • Shorter Project Schedules: The e-house is built and tested in the factory while site civil work proceeds, so the two run in parallel instead of in sequence.
  • Reduced Field Risk: Integration, wiring and most testing happen indoors under controlled conditions, which means less rework, fewer field crews and fewer weather delays.
  • Proven Before Delivery: Factory acceptance testing and off-site commissioning verify the complete system before it ships.
  • Safer by Design: Working clearances, egress, arc-flash venting and fire protection are engineered into the building from the start.
  • Single Point of Accountability: One team from design through integration, testing, NETA site acceptance and handover.

What's Inside a CSI E-House

Typical equipment and systems, configured to your single-line diagram:

  • Power Distribution: Medium-voltage switchgear, dry-type transformers, low-voltage switchgear and bus duct.
  • Motor Control: Motor control centers and variable frequency drives.
  • Protection & Control: Protective relays, SCADA / RTU and operator HMI.
  • Backup Power: UPS, DC chargers and battery systems in a separated, ventilated battery room.
  • Building Systems: Redundant HVAC, cable tray, lighting and emergency lighting, fire detection and clean-agent suppression.

E-House Design & Engineering

Every e-house starts from your single-line diagram. We size the building around the equipment and the people who will work in it, then engineer the structure, building systems and interconnections as one package.

Service Overview: Design & Engineering covers the electrical, structural and building-systems design of the e-house: equipment layout, working clearances and egress, arc-flash venting, HVAC and heat-load sizing, fire detection and suppression, lighting, grounding and cable routing.

CSI Approach: CSI's multidisciplinary team develops the layout from the single-line diagram and equipment data, checks working space and egress against the adopted NEC edition and the equipment manufacturers' requirements, and coordinates the structural skid, lifting points and shipping splits with transport limits from the start.

Value to the project: A building designed around its equipment avoids late re-layouts, oversized footprints and field modifications. Clearances, venting and building systems are resolved on paper, where changes are cheap.

Related FAQs

Factory Integration & Quality Control

Switchgear, MCCs, drives, UPS, batteries and building systems are set, bussed and wired inside the building in a controlled factory environment.

Service Overview: Factory Integration & Quality Control covers equipment setting and anchoring, bus duct and cable installation, control wiring, building-systems installation and the inspections that verify each step against the drawings and the project quality plan.

CSI Approach: CSI's QA/QC engineers inspect torque, clearances, labelling, wiring terminations and construction at defined hold points, record every check, and close non-conformances before the building moves to testing.

Value to the project: Work done indoors is faster, cleaner and easier to inspect than work done in the field. Issues are caught at the bench, not during energization.

Related FAQs

Typical hold points include receipt inspection of equipment, anchoring and alignment, bus and cable termination torque, point-to-point wiring checks, labelling, grounding continuity and building-systems installation checks.

Yes. Inspection and test plans identify witness and hold points, and clients are invited to attend any of them in person or remotely.

Factory Acceptance Testing (FAT)

Before the e-house leaves the factory, the complete assembly is tested as a working system, with your team invited to witness and sign off.

Service Overview: Factory Acceptance Testing verifies that the integrated e-house performs to the specification: insulation and continuity testing, protective relay and interlock testing, control and SCADA functional checks, UPS and DC system testing, and building systems such as HVAC, fire alarm and lighting.

CSI Approach: CSI prepares the FAT procedures from the project specification and drawings, runs the tests with calibrated equipment, records every result, and manages a punch list to closure before shipment is released.

Value to the project: Problems found in the factory cost a fraction of what they cost on site. A witnessed FAT gives the owner confidence and evidence before the building ships.

Related FAQs

Insulation resistance and hi-pot testing, primary and secondary injection of protective relays, breaker and interlock functional tests, control and communication checks, UPS and battery tests, and functional tests of HVAC, lighting and fire detection.

It depends on the size and complexity of the e-house. A single-module building can often be tested in days; multi-module projects with extensive controls take longer. The schedule is agreed in the FAT procedure.

Off-Site Commissioning

Power, controls and building systems are brought up together in the factory, so integration issues are found before the e-house is on a truck.

Service Overview: Off-Site Commissioning energizes and operates the e-house systems as they will run in service, verifying sequences of operation, alarms, communications to the plant control system, and the interaction between electrical and building systems.

CSI Approach: CSI's commissioning engineers apply the same structured process used on our site commissioning work: documented pre-functional checks, functional performance tests, issue tracking and verification of fixes before handover to transport.

Value to the project: Commissioning in the factory shortens site startup, reduces the number of people needed on site and lowers the risk of delays at energization.

Related FAQs

FAT verifies that equipment and assemblies meet the specification. Off-site commissioning goes further and operates the integrated systems together, verifying sequences, alarms and communications as they will run on site.

No. It moves most of the work into the factory, so site commissioning is shorter and focuses on interconnections, site-specific systems and final performance verification.

Site Acceptance & NETA Testing

After transport and setting, the e-house is reconnected, re-tested and energized on site.

Service Overview: Site Acceptance Testing confirms the e-house arrived undamaged and performs as it did in the factory. It includes NETA acceptance testing of the electrical equipment, verification of field interconnections and a supervised first energization.

CSI Approach: CSI's NETA-certified technicians perform acceptance testing to ANSI/NETA ATS, verify the field terminations and interconnections, and coordinate energization with the owner, utility and contractors.

Value to the project: The owner receives independent, standards-based evidence that the equipment is safe and ready for service, and a single team accountable from factory to energization.

Related FAQs

Transport, lifting and setting can disturb connections and equipment. Site acceptance and NETA testing confirm nothing changed and verify the field connections made after delivery.

Electrical acceptance testing is performed to ANSI/NETA ATS, the Standard for Acceptance Testing Specifications for Electrical Power Equipment and Systems, together with the project specification.

Documentation & Handover

The record ships with the building.

Service Overview: Documentation & Handover compiles the test reports, as-built drawings, operation and maintenance manuals, certificates and training material for the e-house into a complete, organized turnover package.

CSI Approach: CSI collects documentation throughout design, integration and testing rather than at the end, reviews it for completeness, and delivers it in the format your facility team uses, with training for operators and maintenance staff.

Value to the project: Complete records make operation, maintenance and future modifications faster and safer, and support warranty and compliance requirements.

Related FAQs

Typically: as-built drawings, FAT and SAT reports, NETA test reports, relay settings, equipment manuals and data sheets, spare parts lists, certificates and training records.

Yes. Turnover packages can be delivered as organized digital files or loaded into the owner's document management or CMMS platform.
